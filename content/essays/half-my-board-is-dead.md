Remember when people asked you as a child: "What do you want to be when you grow up?"

I'm not too fond of this question.

Because I have observed 18-year-olds answering this question,

and I have observed 48-year-olds answering this question.

Both are relatively clueless about what they want to do and the next steps in life.

It is not a dig at the 18-year-old or the 48-year-old.

It is just a dig at a not-so-helpful question.

I propose a better question: Who do you want to be?

I want to be like my board members.

I want to be like the people I admire.

All my board members are exceptional beings who have achieved remarkable feats.

I admire them.

They inspire me,

and I strive to follow their path to success.

Each member is a north star for me to aim towards, a guiding mechanism to help me navigate decisions in my life and business.

I have studied each of my board members deeply.

I have followed their lives.

I have read their books.

I have watched their interviews.

I have tried my best to get inside their heads.

Each board member is there for a reason.

I could write a dedicated post about each of them.

Below is the executive-level summary and the key lessons learned from each member.

---

Elon Musk 🚀Elon, a.k.a. The real-life Tony Stark.

In my eyes, he is a catalyst.

A catalyst for change,

and change that drives us forward

as a planet and a race (the human race).

I read his biography a few years ago, and it was impactful.

I admire his ability to sustain pressure under scrutiny.

Learning about how he navigated the precarious situation amidst multiple failed SpaceX launches was particularly insightful.

He has taught me a lot about entrepreneurship and first principles thinking.

Thank you, Mr Musk.

---

Simon Sinek 🎯Simon Sinek taught me to *Start With Why.*

His teachings transcend marketing.

It's led to a complete reframing of my thinking.

I always *start with why* in everything I do.

If the why isn't solid, there is no point in undertaking further action.

Thank you, Mr Sinek.

---

Leonardo da Vinci 🎨Leonardo da Vinci was born 572 years ago, in 1452.

He is one of the most influential figures of the Renaissance.

His life and work encapsulate a blend of art and science.

The Last Supper (1495 -1498),

Mona Lisa (1503 - 1519),

his study of the human anatomy,

his Codex on the flight of birds,

his engineering designs of bridges, military machines and hydraulic systems,

and the list goes on.

He was the quintessential polymath,

and a man ahead of his time.

He encourages me to be a polymath also,

and to be curious,

very curious,

about life and its constituent parts.

Thank you, Leonardo.

---

Thomas Edison 💡Thomas Edison, the bringer of light.

He invented the light bulb in 1879.

He is not on my board for inventing the light bulb.

Instead, he's on my board because of how he invented the lightbulb.

“I have not failed. I’ve just found 10,000 ways that won’t work.”

Attempting something 10,000 times is a feat, especially when you may not get the results of those attempts and work immediately.

I often think about Edison and how he must have felt on his journey because I see many parallels with entrepreneurship.

Attempt after attempt after attempt, but still no results.

Attempt after attempt after attempt, but still no results.

Attempt after attempt after attempt, more attempts, and still no results.

Attempt, attempt, attempt.. ***boom*** we have light💡

He has taught me grit, resilience, and the power of *giving it one more go.*

Thank you, Mr Edison.

---

Isaac Newton 🍎Isaac Newton, a.k.a. The G.O.A.T of Science.

He lived 381 years ago.

I could create a board comprised of only Scientists,

and you would see some familiar names.

Albert Einstein, Marie-Curie, Charles Darwin, Nikola Tesla, etc.

Isaac Newton stands out for a few reasons.

Namely, the significance of his findings and the time he founded it.

He taught us about the three fundamental laws of motion,

explored the laws of gravity,

and even developed the "methods of fluxions, " later known as calculus.

He did this when "witch trials" were still common.

Can you imagine?

Living in a time when people are being hanged and burnt at the stake for being a "witch",

and you're trying to convince these same people of "science".

It must have been a crazy time to be alive.

Thank you, Mr Newton.

---

Edward Snowden 🧑🏻‍💻Edward Snowden is on my board for his bravery in opposing the National Security Agency (NSA) and for the sacrifices he made for the greater public good.

Edward started his career in the Special Forces in 2004.

In 2006, he began working for the Central Intelligence Agency.

In 2009, he transitioned to the NSA.

There, he became increasingly disillusioned with the agency's surveillance practices.

In 2013, he leaked thousands of documents to *The Guardian* and *The Washington Post.*

The documents revealed the U.S. Government was spying on its people and collecting metadata on its population - without them knowing.

As a result of leaking these documents, he had to uproot his life, and he sought Asylum in Hong Kong and Russia.

He acted out of a moral obligation to inform the public about the government's overreach.

In doing so, he sacrificed his home in the U.S.

I appreciate you, Edward. Thank you for your bravery.

---

Galileo Galilei 🔭During my GSCEs, I worked extremely hard.

So hard that I was miles ahead of the curriculum.

While my class was halfway through the term, I had already finished it.

It enabled me to take an extra GCSE in Astronomy.

Exploring the vast universe and our place in it blew my mind.

My Saturday mornings revolved around commuting to the Greenwich Observatory.

I learnt about so many fascinating topics - one of them was the life of Galileo.

His contributions to astronomy are astounding.

He improved the design of the telescope, enabling magnification up to 30x.

With this, he gathered empirical data that challenged much of the conventional thought of that time.

One such evidence was the discovery of Jupiter's moons.

Back then, we believed everything revolved around the earth (pretty self-centred of us humans if you ask me),

but his findings undermined the geocentric model of the universe.

Instead, it supported the idea of a heliocentric model,

where the sun was the centre of the solar system, not the earth.

A fact we take for granted in present times.

Can you imagine how fundamental this change is?

We used to think everything revolved around the earth.

This man proved that it didn't.

It was an earth-shattering discovery that changed our universe and our place in it.

I appreciate you, Galileo.

---

David Goggins 🏃🏾‍♂️David Goggin's story is inspirational,

not because of where he is now or who he is now,

but because of where he came from and who he was.

From being abused by his Dad,

being overweight,

depressed,

and killing cockroaches for a living,

to being one of the hardest humans to exist.

He is a shining example of what is possible when you refuse to quit.

I've learned a lot from studying his life and understanding his story.

The 40% rule,

conducting After Action Reports,

and dipping into the cookie jar of past victories are some of the ideas I think about often.

David inspired me to run 100km every month this year - one of the best decisions of my life.

Thank you, David.

P.S. Let's get a workout in together one day!

---

Marcus Aurelius 🧘🏽Marcus Aurelius - what can I say about this man?

He probably saved my life.

I owe him so much, and there is no way for me to repay him.

But I will try my best by relaying his message and building on his thoughts.

I often think about the continuity of life.

What would a person be thinking and doing 100 years ago?

What would a person be thinking and doing 200 years ago?

What would a person be thinking and doing 500 years ago?

What would a person be thinking and doing 1000 years ago?

What would a person be thinking and doing 2000 years ago? Lucky for me, Marcus put his thoughts down in his *Meditations*.

I find it uncanny how his observations from 1923 years ago still apply today.

Sometimes, it feels like he is reaching out from the past to guide me in the present.

Thank you, Marcus.

---

Rosa Parks 🚌Rosa Parks - the mother of the Civil Rights Movement.

She is on my board for her remarkable bravery during the Montgomery Bus Boycott.

We're easy to forget that racism was the norm only a couple of decades ago.

It would still be the norm if it were not for civil rights leaders like her, Martin Luther King and Malcolm X.

During the 1950s, public transport was segregated. White people sat in the front, and black people sat in the back.

On December 1st 1955, on her way home from work, she sat where the white people sat.

The bus driver ordered her to move, but she refused.

She refused to move.

She stood up for her rights by remaining seated.

She was arrested, convicted and fined.

But more importantly, her arrest sparked the Montgomery bus boycott.

On November 13th 1956, after 381 days of boycotting, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled segregation on public buses unconstitutional.

To me, she is a symbol of resistance.

She's taught me the importance of standing up for yourself and your rights - no matter the cost.

I often find myself questioning my life decisions about being an entrepreneur.

After all, it is so much easier to pursue traditional employment.

But just as Rosa resisted the easy option of following the norm,

I choose to resist and say no.

Thank you, Mrs Park.

---

Gary Vaynerchuk 🍷Gary Vaynerchuk is on my board for his authenticity and humility.

I have followed and studied his journey for a while now.

Like a sailor in stormy seas looking for a lighthouse in the middle of the night,

he is a beacon of light for any entrepreneur looking to make a living doing what they love.

His message and approach are refreshing, and his positivity is infectious.

As a 28-year-old entrepreneur, I can only confirm two things:

I'm on the grind.

And I haven't made it yet.

To this end, Gary was also in the same spot back in 2006 when he started WineLibraryTV at the age of 31.

Gary produced an episode almost every day for 5 years, and his hard work paid off.

He deserves every ounce of it.

Gary has taught me the importance of content and patience.

The only reason you're reading this is because of Gary.

Thank you, Gary.

---

Lebron James 🏀You might think having Lebron James on my board is a bit strange.

You may assume it is because of the four rings he's won,

but that is not the reason.

To me, he exemplifies excellence, consistency and longevity.

Lebron is 39 years old and considered eight years past his prime, as most NBA players peak between 27 and 31 years.

He is so past his prime that he is playing with his son Bronny James on the Lakers this season.

The first father-son duo to grace the league - wild.

The ungodly thing about Lebron is his average numbers over his 22 seasons in the league.

27.1 points, 7.5 rebounds, 7.4 assists.

Basketball is his craft, and he is damn good at it.

I observe his approach and habits when he is not working on his craft, Particularly his emphasis on rest and recovery.

I aspire to match his physical excellence when I reach 39.

Thank you, Lebron.

---

Bruce Lee 🌊Bruce Lee is the board member that I have known the longest.

I grew up watching Bruce Lee movies in Nepal.

Enter the Dragon and Way of the Dragon were my favourites.

But he's not on here for his acting career.

He is on here because he *taught me to be water.*

"Empty your mind. Be formless, shapeless, like water. You put water into a cup; it becomes the cup. You put water into a bottle; it becomes the bottle. You put it in a teapot; it becomes the teapot. Now water can flow or it can crash. Be water, my friend."

When I was younger, I didn't fully comprehend what he meant by this.

I only understood it after an identity crisis in my mid-20s.

Being water is profound.

It taught me to be formless and adaptable in thought and action.

I love how Bruce developed Jeet Kune Do (JKD) in 1967.

Before Bruce, each fighting style had distinct techniques and principles, but he integrated those techniques.

He took the boxing footwork inspired by Muhammad Ali,

Wing Chun Kung Fu techniques from his training under Yip Man

and many others to create his technique - JKD.

He wasn't confined to one technique or one identity.

He found his way and made his technique.

I apply this approach to entrepreneurship by combining all startup principles (marketing, sales, engineering, etc.) to be a full-stack founder.
