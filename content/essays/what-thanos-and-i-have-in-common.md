It was the date that Avengers Endgame came out.

A crazy time to be alive if you're a Marvel fan.

I've never seen more excitement for a movie,

and this excitement was reflected in the movie's opening weekend.

It broke records and earned over $1.2 billion globally in ticket sales.

The whole film was a highlight reel, but one moment stands out.

During the final battle, Captain America stares down Thanos' army.

He is battered and bruised.

His shield is broken,

and all seems lost.

One man against a whole army.

In the face of overwhelming odds, he doesn't cower, though.

He straps his fragmented shield tighter to his forearms and prepares for his last stand.

Then, he hears a whisper of a message.

It's the other Avengers who had perished from *the snap*.

They return through Doctor Strange's portal, and then it happens.

Captain America attracts Thor's hammer and announces - "Avengers… assemble!"

It was an all-time great moment in cinematic history.

I get goosebumps just thinking about it.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mTz5!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F71d52deb-43ec-480d-ab6d-a014c33842ee_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mTz5!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F71d52deb-43ec-480d-ab6d-a014c33842ee_2862x388.png)Avengers was a great movie because of Thanos,

the antihero, 

the antagonist.

He is a complex, multi-dimensional character.

Imagining him as a hero in another universe is easy.

Thanos' home world - Titan, was destroyed by overpopulation.

This drives him towards his objective.

To erase half of all life in the universe.

Why?

By eliminating half of all life,

he ensures the survival and prosperity of the remaining population.

He thinks his actions are for the greater good.

He must acquire 6 infinity stones scattered across the universe to achieve his objective.

The Space Stone,

Mind Stone,

Reality Stone,

Power Stone,

Time Stone

and the Soul Stone.

All these stones govern a particular aspect of the universe.

The more stones you have, the more powerful you are.

Thanos searched the universe for these stones.

Slowly but surely, he acquired them one at a time.

Each one helps him acquire the next one.

He gets all 6 and becomes the most powerful being in the universe.

He is so powerful that he erases half of the population and achieves his objective with a finger snap.

It takes all of the Avengers to bring him down and undo his actions,

but they don't all make it out alive.

R.I.P Iron Man.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!m3Y8!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9243ef1b-aef7-415b-aa08-6102b9d8048c_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!m3Y8!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9243ef1b-aef7-415b-aa08-6102b9d8048c_2862x388.png)Just like Thanos searched for the infinity stones to make his vision a reality,

I have also been searching for something over the last 7 years.

I have been searching for skills.

Skills to make my vision a reality.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wYsF!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8a1c4f1a-d69a-4098-bb4a-3219f3e251c0_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wYsF!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8a1c4f1a-d69a-4098-bb4a-3219f3e251c0_2862x388.png)Each infinity stone governs a particular aspect of the universe.

Each one gives Thanos a unique power.

I think about skills in the same way.

Each skill you possess gives you a specific power - a particular ability.

Skills unlock everything:

Your speed.

Your independence.

Your control.

Your adaptability.

Your true potential.

**"Give a man a fish, and you feed him for a day; teach a man to fish, and you feed him for a lifetime"**

-Ancient Chinese Proverb

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ZvKo!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb98c1e8f-7d3e-4b42-83d9-687ef6d426b1_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ZvKo!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb98c1e8f-7d3e-4b42-83d9-687ef6d426b1_2862x388.png)When you think about what makes a successful business or a startup, 

it is not the product, the idea, or the processes.

It is the people.

These people possess skills and attributes.

These skills and attributes help them execute their function.

The unique combinations of skills and attributes lead to the development of the product, the idea and the processes.

---

Examples:

If you have a skilled marketers team. They will create attention-grabbing campaigns, and your startup will get much attention.

If you have a skilled technical team, they will create an excellent, technically sound product, and you'll be able to iterate quickly.

If you have a skilled operational team, everyone in the company will know their roles and have everything they need to do their job efficiently.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!zZfB!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F26704dc2-48ac-4e98-9b4f-7fce7fee50d7_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!zZfB!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F26704dc2-48ac-4e98-9b4f-7fce7fee50d7_2862x388.png)If you want to start and run a successful startup, operate with the end in mind.

Look at some of the most successful companies in the world and observe their company structure.

They have distinct business divisions:

Sales

Engineering

Finance

Marketing

Product

Design

Operations

Strategy

Legal

Human Resources

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!04x3!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2d0a4aba-1791-4523-b8b7-52abb33b96e8_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!04x3!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2d0a4aba-1791-4523-b8b7-52abb33b96e8_2862x388.png)Your company is a living organism, much like the human body.

Each business division has a purpose and a particular function.

Each division is specialised for a particular task. 

Examples:

The sales team is responsible for generating sales.

The engineering team is responsible for programming the product.

The product team is responsible for designing the product. 

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!gkzv!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5988f3bf-7f07-40ff-8bc4-969dcb8e1565_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!gkzv!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5988f3bf-7f07-40ff-8bc4-969dcb8e1565_2862x388.png)In the early days of your startup, you need to competently perform across all of these functions.

To do so, you need to develop your skill set. 

Your ability to execute across these functions will determine whether you're successful or not. 

For example:

You have nothing to market if you're a skilled marketer who can't build your product.

If you're a skilled developer who cannot talk to customers or market your product, you have a product that no one cares about.

As your startup grows, you'll have the resources to hire people to perform each of these specialised functions.

Ideally, these hires will be better than you at that particular function. 

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TcQP!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe88e458a-ec4b-4265-bb84-d96f423f450a_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TcQP!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe88e458a-ec4b-4265-bb84-d96f423f450a_2862x388.png)So what skillset do you need?

The skills and attributes vary by business division:

Sales: you need to be outgoing, persuasive and thick-skinned.

Engineering: you need to think programmatically to solve complex problems.

Finance: you need analytical and financial modelling skills.

Marketing: you need creativity to create a brand that gets attention.

Product: you need to research skills and empathy to think like a user.

Design: you need creativity, bravery and attention to detail.

Operations: you need to be freakishly organised.

Strategy: you need to see the bigger picture without getting lost in it.

Legal: you need patience, precision and attention to detail.

Human Resources: you need to be a good judge of character.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sSEM!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F45f3a687-c60f-4a7b-a9fe-e6d85e97b724_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sSEM!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F45f3a687-c60f-4a7b-a9fe-e6d85e97b724_2862x388.png)Here are some scenarios that highlight the importance of the distribution of these skills:

---

**Example 1:**

Take two solo founders who are both building their own startups.

They are identical except for one fact.

Solo Founder A has no skills across any of these divisions,

and Solo Founder B has skills across all of them.

**Which one is more likely to succeed with their startup?**

**Solo Founder A**: with more skills, they will likely succeed because they can execute their startup idea more effectively.

---

**Example 2:**

Two founding teams are building their own startup.

Founding Team A has two founders with different skill sets.

Founding Team B has two founders with homogeneous skill sets. 

**Which one is more likely to succeed with their startup?**

**Founding Team A**: the value of a collectively diversified skillset is much larger than the homogeneous skill sets of Founding Team B. 

Furthermore, having two people with the same skillset also raises other issues. 

Imagine you're in Founding Team B.

You and your co-founder are both salespeople. 

If you feel you can do a better job than your co-founder, you will slowly lose respect for your co-founder over time. 

I've seen this happen to so many startup teams. 

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WGSk!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9f2740a3-f830-4a87-bcda-886265cecd4c_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WGSk!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9f2740a3-f830-4a87-bcda-886265cecd4c_2862x388.png)These two examples highlight a few truths:

More skills are better than less.

In a team, the collective diversity in skills is preferred over homogeneity.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!x9uq!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2c45eabc-00e0-41c7-bb2c-d9b19e954d97_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!x9uq!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2c45eabc-00e0-41c7-bb2c-d9b19e954d97_2862x388.png)If Thanos was a startup founder, he'd be a *Full-stack Founder*.

A Founder with all the skill sets to make his visions a reality. 

A Founder who is highly skilled, adaptable and resourceful.

We can all learn from Thanos.

We all crave the power to make our visions a reality, and the most effective way to do this is by acquiring and developing skill sets. 

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!-3z_!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7544c01f-734d-4582-ae3e-24d6c816e56d_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!-3z_!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7544c01f-734d-4582-ae3e-24d6c816e56d_2862x388.png)I've always had this in mind over the last seven years, and [my story](https://deckandadream.substack.com/p/my-story) reflect this.

It's affected how I've navigated my career and extra-curricular activities.

I have optimised for the breadth of experiences while achieving a decent depth in each division.

Through my full-time positions, I've developed my skillset in sales, marketing, strategy and operations.

I have supplemented this with my extra-curricular activities.

Through my first startup, PlanIt, I developed my product skillset.

I learnt design principles and got comfortable transforming ideas on a napkin into high-fidelity wireframes.

After my time at PitchBook, I built an app called Pesto.io - a sales enablement tool, using Bubble (a no-code tool), which helped me develop my engineering skillset.

Earlier this year, I formalised my engineering skillset by undertaking *Harvard's CS50 - Introduction to Computer Science* course, which taught me programming fundamentals.

While at R3, I worked on a startup called Blockarch. This was the first time I worked with three other co-founders.

I deliberately conceded the CEO position to become the Chief Operating Officer because I wanted more talent and process management exposure.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!tMdU!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F181f7603-0a60-488d-a334-ffa04da01fb3_2862x388.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!tMdU!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F181f7603-0a60-488d-a334-ffa04da01fb3_2862x388.png)All in all, acquiring these skills has been empowering.

I can't snap my fingers and instantly build my startup; it still takes time and a lot of execution, 

but I have the blueprint and the refined skillsets through thousands of hours of practice.

I know what is required, and I know I can execute effectively.

Acquiring these skills has also given me a lot of options. 

Before, I had a sense of desperation.

I settled for freelancers, co-founders, and team members because they had relevant and complementary skill sets.

I did not feel competent enough to execute independently, so I was either:

willing to work on ideas in domains that didn't interest me;

or trying to sell my ideas to someone else and convince them to work with me.

That's not the case anymore.

I'm a highly interoperable founder now. 

I choose who I work with and the ideas I work on.

I work with people I care about and fit around their skill sets.

This, in turn, has helped me [engineer a perfect life](https://deckandadream.substack.com/p/engineering-a-perfect-life).
