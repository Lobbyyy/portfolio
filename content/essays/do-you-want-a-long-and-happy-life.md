The unplanned, unstructured nature of it.

You walk in, and you have no idea what you will walk out with.

A couple years back, I walked into Waterstones and picked up a book.

It was called ikigai.

They say they don't judge a book by its cover, but I did.

The cover featured a blossom tree and the short title "The Japanese Secret to a Long and Happy Life."

As someone who wants to live a long and happy life, how could I not purchase the book?

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0HW2!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F285a0638-dd0a-4240-bb08-5a3f9dd3775a_1468x171.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0HW2!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F285a0638-dd0a-4240-bb08-5a3f9dd3775a_1468x171.png)The book introduced me to new ideas that changed my perspective on life, 

and I don't say that lightly.

The first was the idea of wabi-sabi - 'the beauty of age and imperfection'.

The second was the idea of being 80% full - eating till you're only 80% full.

The third was the concept of ikigai - 'the happiness of always being busy'.

Each idea is significant and deserves its own post.

This post aims to introduce you to the third idea - ikigai.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!T1zm!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F81e28bec-b3df-4c12-9e8f-47c52160b502_1468x171.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!T1zm!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F81e28bec-b3df-4c12-9e8f-47c52160b502_1468x171.png)**Subscribe for access to practical business frameworks and startup insights! 🚀**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9NVQ!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe9438d7e-b63d-4b69-8539-bda192a8f13f_1468x171.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9NVQ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe9438d7e-b63d-4b69-8539-bda192a8f13f_1468x171.png)The book ikigai is often cited as a 'self-improvement book'.

I agree.

It does help you improve yourself,

but not as much as it enables you to discover yourself.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!yP-Q!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0a6c9da9-ca91-40cd-96da-49ae8e322197_1468x171.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!yP-Q!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0a6c9da9-ca91-40cd-96da-49ae8e322197_1468x171.png)The core idea is that there are 4 spheres of life.

Each sphere plays a critical part in your life and serves a particular purpose.

*1st sphere = what you love*

*2nd sphere = what you're good at*

*3rd sphere = what you can be paid for*

*4th sphere = what the world needs*

In each sphere, you write down the things that belong to that sphere.

Once the spheres are populated, you look for patterns.

**The goal is to find something across all four spheres - this is your ikigai.**

As a simple example, here is a non-exhaustive list of how I populate my spheres:

---

**What you love**

Writing

Learning

Meeting new people

Going to the gym

Building tech companies

---

**What you're good at**

Learning

Meeting new people

Being fit

Building tech companies

---

**What you can be paid for**

Building tech companies

Creating Go-to-Market (GTM) strategies

Crafting pitch decks

Problem-solving

Going to the gym/being fit

---

**What the world needs**

Peace

Love

Empathy

Tech that brings people together

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!u2dt!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbe0606a5-7c3e-4f20-b4e8-d062a7fc480c_1468x171.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!u2dt!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbe0606a5-7c3e-4f20-b4e8-d062a7fc480c_1468x171.png)Once you’ve populated all the spheres, start looking for overlaps.

Are there certain things that are in two spheres?

Are there certain things that are in three spheres?

Are there certain things that are in all four spheres?

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!SmCp!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7646c7a8-b370-4df2-87d0-37f9ed714d93_1468x171.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!SmCp!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7646c7a8-b370-4df2-87d0-37f9ed714d93_1468x171.png)The overlaps are an important signal.

It signals transcendence.

Something that can't be contained just in one sphere.

If you consider a Venn diagram, the overlaps are simply the intersection between spheres.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!h8wp!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff8ffe6ff-1117-426d-b2f0-20db40213f01_1468x171.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!h8wp!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff8ffe6ff-1117-426d-b2f0-20db40213f01_1468x171.png)Observing my spheres, here are the overlaps:

1 overlap (*across two spheres*)

Meeting new people *(what I love + what I'm good at)*

Learning *(what I love + what I'm good at)*

2 overlaps *(across three spheres)*

Going to the gym/being fit *(what I love + what I'm good at + what I can be paid for)*

3 overlaps* (across four spheres)*

Building tech companies *(all spheres)*

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mHv-!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F53fa9eda-b586-4697-8b92-f4fbbf44b7c4_1468x171.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mHv-!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F53fa9eda-b586-4697-8b92-f4fbbf44b7c4_1468x171.png)In exploring the idea of ikigai, I learnt about myself. I discovered:

who I am,

what I care about,

what I can monetise,

and the impact I want to have on the world.

Ikigai is a map.

Not the type of map that shows you the terrain or the topology of an area,

but a map of *self*.

It is your personal map, and you should leverage it to live a long and happy life.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vUr-!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F300640d2-52fe-437a-86a8-9ad7747ca2fb_1468x171.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vUr-!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F300640d2-52fe-437a-86a8-9ad7747ca2fb_1468x171.png)My ikigai is building tech companies.

I'm curious,

what is your ikigai?
