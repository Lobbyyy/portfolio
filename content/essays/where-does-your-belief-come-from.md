Q1. Why do you think you have what it takes to be an entrepreneur?
Q2. Why do you think you can succeed where most fail?

Here are my answers.[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!FDyN!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9d1b8945-9ff6-4f4c-91fe-f95b14f6dc20_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!FDyN!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9d1b8945-9ff6-4f4c-91fe-f95b14f6dc20_2394x340.png)Entrepreneurship is an uphill battle.

Luckily for me,

[my whole life](/essays/my-story) has been an uphill battle.

The cards have *always* been stacked against me,

and I revel in this fact.

The more impossible something feels,

the more I want to do it.

To prove to myself that I can.

To prove that anything is possible.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!GIpm!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5857a61d-0fec-4dd4-a119-042800e7846f_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!GIpm!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5857a61d-0fec-4dd4-a119-042800e7846f_2394x340.png)During my GCSEs and A-Levels,

I fought for my life to make it to a good university.

I went to a public school in South London.

It wasn't known for its education.

It was infamous for roadmen,

fights, stabbings,

and its general poor academic performance.

Think *Top Boy* — minus the guns.

It is an all-boys school,

and when I recall my experience there,

I keep thinking of a scene from *Lord of the Flies*.

The scene I'm referring to is the one when Piggy's conch gets smashed by Roger.

I actually remember studying and writing about this in my English Literature class.

How ironic that I'm here writing about it again, 12 years later.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!E76W!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F090561fe-da93-4273-9dd5-b4d59e27d88b_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!E76W!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F090561fe-da93-4273-9dd5-b4d59e27d88b_2394x340.png)If you haven't read Lord of the Flies, here is a quick summary:

A bunch of young kids become stranded on an island,

At the start, it was fun.

An island with no adults means they can do whatever they want.

Over time, the fun turns sour.

The group divides, and tribes start to form,

conflict, barbarianism and death follow.

I won't spoil the end for you.

It's a good read.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!inxT!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F037c9b59-7b25-47f7-9380-9280258e878c_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!inxT!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F037c9b59-7b25-47f7-9380-9280258e878c_2394x340.png)The scene where the conch breaks is significant.

It is a turning point in the book.

It is symbolic because the conch represents order and civilisation

and when it gets destroyed,

it foreshadows the chaos that's about to ensue.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sNVy!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Faeea2065-e9d6-4a33-a102-966a8d8fe1d6_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sNVy!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Faeea2065-e9d6-4a33-a102-966a8d8fe1d6_2394x340.png)My secondary school experience felt the same way.

Hostile,

combative,

out of control,

and unruly.

The conch was already broken before I got there.

I remember so much.

One instance still baffles me to this day.

I was walking to my art class after the first break and saw a kid from the year above coming the other way.

As I walk past him, he grabs my shoulders with both hands and knees me in my chest.

It all happened so fast.

Why did he do that?

I do not even know this guy.

He just casually walks off like nothing happened.

I was more confused than hurt.

He was much bigger and taller than me,

so there was very little I could do.

This was a relatively mild experience in the grand scheme of things.

It would qualify as *Lemon &amp; Herb *spiciness level in *Nandos*.

I know some students who got the *Extra Hot *treatment,

and they got it raw.

These instances have redefined my definition of bullying.

It wasn't pleasant.

It was ugly.

They got violated.

For who they were,

for how they looked,

for how they spoke,

for what they knew,

for what they didn't know,

for what their parents did.

Revolting.

I can't shake those visuals from my memory.

I know our teenage experiences shape us as adults,

and I can't help but wonder how these students are doing now.

I hope they discover ways to deal with the trauma in a healthy way.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VCiC!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa52126df-f1ee-4b83-88fa-474108764ae6_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VCiC!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa52126df-f1ee-4b83-88fa-474108764ae6_2394x340.png)The teachers tried their best, but there were more of *us* than them.

Every class had characters.

The class jokers,

the disrupters,

the fighters,

the drug dealers,

the instigators.

There was a clear pattern to the distribution of these characters.

There were a few in the top set.

As you went down the sets, the number of characters increased.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Iyhq!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F653b40fc-9dd8-421b-bf85-0bd9ecf7956b_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Iyhq!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F653b40fc-9dd8-421b-bf85-0bd9ecf7956b_2394x340.png)I was in the top set for everything.

This was not by luck.

It was by design.

I was incredibly studious, and I had to be.

That was the only way I would 'make it out' the ends.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TbYd!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F070374e9-efb1-4418-9354-7f77e43145e7_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TbYd!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F070374e9-efb1-4418-9354-7f77e43145e7_2394x340.png)I could foresee the career path of the average student from my school

or more accurately, I couldn't foresee anything.

I don't know what was scarier.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vkBc!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdf9c8a40-a3eb-4f73-a464-ce9e7247d7f6_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vkBc!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdf9c8a40-a3eb-4f73-a464-ce9e7247d7f6_2394x340.png)From an early age, I knew following the crowd was not an option.

Because that would get me nowhere.

So I decided.

I decided I wanted to make something of myself.

I worked extremely hard.

I did as much as I could,

and I did it to the best of my abilities.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!zYkA!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7821eb3f-cedf-4bbd-b111-3cfca394506e_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!zYkA!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7821eb3f-cedf-4bbd-b111-3cfca394506e_2394x340.png)I studied 13 subjects for my GCSEs, 

including additional ones like Astronomy and Additional Maths.

I got 9 A*’s and 4 A’s.

I was on the fence about what to study at university.

Either Economics at LSE or Maths at Cambridge.

I found both very attractive.

I loved the real-world aspect of economics

and I loved how there was always an absolute answer in maths

and the answer never involved writing words.


[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5JSb!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9bf5b557-8695-4c68-abc5-d74378d118ab_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5JSb!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9bf5b557-8695-4c68-abc5-d74378d118ab_2394x340.png)I started my AS year with 4 subjects - Biology, Chemistry, Maths and Economics.

I knew getting into Cambridge and LSE would be challenging because of the competition.

No one from my school had made it there before - not in recent history, anyway.

I was competing with kids from private schools and boarding schools;

kids with way more access to resources and connections than me.

I wasn't scared, though. I wanted the challenge.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Zbwg!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F47c66548-e1ba-4d54-81c0-bb3854e0ade0_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Zbwg!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F47c66548-e1ba-4d54-81c0-bb3854e0ade0_2394x340.png)2 months before my AS exams in March 2014,

I decided to do something crazy.

I picked up another AS Level - Further Maths.

Can you imagine?

The exam is in two months,

and I haven't even started the curriculum yet.

I hadn’t even opened the textbook yet.

I didn't even have the textbook.

Most of my teachers advised against it.

They thought this decision would hinder my performance in my other subjects.

They said it was impossible.

To make matters more challenging, I couldn't join the Further Maths class because my existing timetable was incompatible with it.

It was no surprise - I was already doing 4 AS subjects, and I only had a few free gaps in my timetable.

If I was going to do this, I knew I was on my own.

I had to learn and study the curriculum myself.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!MLm8!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5108ec5d-fee7-4d07-abb8-549fbb185a59_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!MLm8!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5108ec5d-fee7-4d07-abb8-549fbb185a59_2394x340.png)Thankfully, I did have one thing going for me.

An amazing Maths teacher.

She was willing to see me before and after school to help me through my blockers.

She could sense my hunger and my desire to make something of myself.

She believed in me, and in fact, she challenged me to do it.

I looked up to her.

She studied Maths at Cambridge, and she was very intelligent.

I wanted to be like her on some level.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!o4j6!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1798cec8-cb2d-42b0-bbc3-0c2e72b43e07_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!o4j6!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1798cec8-cb2d-42b0-bbc3-0c2e72b43e07_2394x340.png)I had a plan to achieve the impossible.

For the next two months, I’d sit in her class after school till 5:30 doing further maths.

Then, I’d go home and do work for my other AS levels.

By 9-10pm, I’d start further maths again.

And I would go till 2-3 am consistently.

Then, I’d wake up for school at 8am and repeat.

I did this every day for 2 months.

Looking back on those two months,

I went into* Goggins mode*.

Mind you, this was before David Goggins

was David Goggins.

I was under extreme pressure; it felt like my whole future was hanging in the balance.

The stakes were high.

I had to perform in the exams,

and I had to 'make it'.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5c28!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F977ad3e0-92a7-4906-81de-3930f0eefce1_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5c28!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F977ad3e0-92a7-4906-81de-3930f0eefce1_2394x340.png)The two months came by and flew by,

I sat my further maths exams,

I took my other AS exams, 

and I got A*'s and A's across the board.

I was over the moon, especially with my further maths results.

I was the only student to get an A that year,

even though the rest of the class had 6 additional months of lessons.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vEO-!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fcbd06ee4-ed95-4603-99e1-254badc9aa73_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vEO-!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fcbd06ee4-ed95-4603-99e1-254badc9aa73_2394x340.png)This experience was an inflection point in my life.

It taught me so many important life lessons, which I still reflect upon to this day.

It taught me that I shouldn't listen to advice.

All these teachers were older than me,

they knew so much more about life,

they had so many more experiences than me,

they had so much knowledge about their subject,

and yet, 

they were wrong.

All my teachers except for my maths teacher advised against it.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Vj8t!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb292f9ca-bcf2-46aa-898c-9220bb4662a5_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Vj8t!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb292f9ca-bcf2-46aa-898c-9220bb4662a5_2394x340.png)As I reflect on this experience as a 28-year-old, I realise why they were wrong.

Those teachers were once kids, and their experiences shaped them.

Maybe they believed in themselves; maybe they didn't.

Maybe they wanted to be teachers; maybe they just fell into teaching.

Regardless, those teachers weren't me.

They couldn't see the world through my eyes.

They couldn't experience the desire and hunger I felt.

They didn't know what I was willing to give to get what I wanted.

They had a partial understanding of who I was. 

Their data points were limited; their understanding was partial.

Those teachers didn't know my abilities,

they didn't know what I wanted, and

They didn’t know how badly I wanted it.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2GJI!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0ff4af63-b214-4ae5-9e83-bbe10f923d2d_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2GJI!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0ff4af63-b214-4ae5-9e83-bbe10f923d2d_2394x340.png)It taught me that 'impossible’ is just a word.

It is so easily thrown about and accepted.

It makes people feel safe.

It is comforting to know something is impossible because it warrants no further thinking.

---

It taught me that through hard work and dedication, anything is possible.

I had to work smart, and I had to work hard,

really hard.

---

It taught me to make my own rules and set my own standards.

No one else dared to do 5 AS's,

I was the only one crazy enough to attempt and succeed in doing it.

---

Most importantly,

it taught me that there is no better feeling than overcoming impossible odds.

No better feeling than having slain the unslayable dragon

and lived to tell the tale.


[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!YvVa!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb6763487-ab80-40c1-b407-f285e8b3b6af_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!YvVa!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb6763487-ab80-40c1-b407-f285e8b3b6af_2394x340.png)If you're thinking about embarking on a daunting journey,

you need to believe you can see that journey through to its end.

It doesn't matter if it's starting a business,

or moving to a new country,

or starting a new job,

or trying to get into a good uni,

or trying to stay sober,

or trying to unlock the secrets of the universe,

or being a parent,

or leaving a toxic relationship,

or trying to be more healthy.

They are all journeys.

Believe you can complete those journeys.

"We all need small sparks, small accomplishments in our lives to fuel the big ones [...] Because its the small sparks, which start small fires, that eventually build enough heat to burn the whole f*cking forest down."

David Goggins, [my board member](/essays/half-my-board-is-dead)

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Av2P!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb0374cdd-eab3-4f5d-9796-5f13dfa0a45b_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Av2P!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb0374cdd-eab3-4f5d-9796-5f13dfa0a45b_2394x340.png)If you're struggling with believing in yourself,

it's okay.

It's a process.

Do you know something that helps?

Evidence.

Evidence that you've successfully completed daunting journeys before.

**If you've done daunting journeys before, surely you can do it again?**

Make a list of all the daunting journeys you have been on;

the journeys you are proud of;

and the things you've accomplished.

If you don't have a list of daunting journeys,

then start a daunting journey and build the belief on the way.

It doesn't matter if you think your accomplishments are small and insignificant to others; it only matters that they are significant to you.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!nBng!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F37686a3f-f3d3-4f3b-a84f-b66b593eb14f_2394x340.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!nBng!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F37686a3f-f3d3-4f3b-a84f-b66b593eb14f_2394x340.png)Text within this block will maintain its original spacing when published**Q1:** Why do you think you have what it takes to be an entrepreneur?
**Answer:** Because I believe I can.

**Q2:** Why do you think you can succeed where most fail?
