[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ytyw!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F475ad3b3-ba51-4a26-92a7-0a1fba2d6c27_2856x272.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ytyw!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F475ad3b3-ba51-4a26-92a7-0a1fba2d6c27_2856x272.png)I was over at my friend's place the other day,

we ordered Popeyes on Deliveroo.

Popeyes is my guilty pleasure.

It's a love-hate relationship.

I love it cause it tastes so good.

The crispiness,

the spiciness,

the flakiness,

hmmm.

I'm not too fond of it because of how I feel the day after.

I feel my stomach curling like it's in the gym.

My stomach suddenly turns into prime Arnold Schwarzenegger,

and unfortunately for me,

its Tuesday at Gold Gym on Venice Beach,

and it's arm day.

Yep, it's terrible.

Something goes wrong in the digestion process.

It is what it is.

I make my bed, and I sleep in it.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!V2fy!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd9cb68b3-19b3-4392-a628-b0435984e99e_2856x272.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!V2fy!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd9cb68b3-19b3-4392-a628-b0435984e99e_2856x272.png)As I bite into those tender wings,

I apologise to my gut bacteria.

The little army of microorganisms in my gut.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_vgu!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb6674426-47a3-4a0b-b97e-712cd1639bd6_2856x272.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_vgu!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb6674426-47a3-4a0b-b97e-712cd1639bd6_2856x272.png)They are the unsung heroes of our bodies,

they receive no love.

If I stopped a random person on the street and asked them about their gut bacteria,

how much would they know about it?

Not much.

Beyond the obvious connection to eating more yoghurt and kaffir,

Their knowledge would be limited.

We overlook the significance of our gut bacteria,

but that little army has a purpose

and it's doing a lot.

Its functions include digestion and nutrient absorption,

immune system regulation,

supporting brain health,

supporting heart health,

blood sugar control,

and vitamin production.

You wouldn't be alive without gut bacteria,

not for long, anyway.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!QV99!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6dd15e19-c660-4da9-9420-a98c7299e836_2856x272.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!QV99!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6dd15e19-c660-4da9-9420-a98c7299e836_2856x272.png)There are approximately 1000 different bacterial species in the gut.

They are tiny,

microscopic,

minuscule,

and unnoticeable.

Usually ranging from 0.2 to 10 micrometres (μm).

That's 0.00002 cm to 0.001 cm.

The smallest object visible to the human eye is 0.01 cm.

If I could describe it with an emoji, it would be 🤏🏼

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!3L0J!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa979ea04-87be-4ded-b7aa-b968de243fc8_2856x272.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!3L0J!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa979ea04-87be-4ded-b7aa-b968de243fc8_2856x272.png)The funny thing is when you consider the scale of the universe.

We humans are the exact same as the gut bacteria.

Tiny microorganisms.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!i1qY!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8a0f19b2-9abb-4612-bd3f-81b9aab168b4_2856x272.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!i1qY!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8a0f19b2-9abb-4612-bd3f-81b9aab168b4_2856x272.png)We are approximately 1.7 million times larger than these tiny micro-organisms.

**How does it feel to be 1.7 million times bigger than something?**

Something about that is appealing.

Maybe it strokes our human ego,

Perhaps we feel safe,

Maybe we know it can't really hurt us.

It's a confirmation of our dominance.

Kings and Queens of Earth.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VpXO!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdc279cf2-cdf1-438f-8607-46d90ec5f0df_2856x272.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VpXO!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdc279cf2-cdf1-438f-8607-46d90ec5f0df_2856x272.png)The universe is quick to serve us some humble pie, though,

and it's the only thing on the menu.

It's operating on a completely different scale.

Unimaginably big and in all directions.

Everywhere.

Anywhere.

All at once.

The universe is approximately 93 billion light years in diameter.

And 1 light year is 9.46 trillion km wide.

If you're wondering..

93 billion light-years x 9.46 trillion km/light-year x 1000m/km = 8.8 x 10^26 meters

**8,800,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000 meters**

so, 

the universe is 52 octillion times larger than us.

That’s 52, followed by 26 zero’s.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!gJXK!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0ec4d7c2-630b-4e5b-a358-fd2a9d8a7a37_2856x272.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!gJXK!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0ec4d7c2-630b-4e5b-a358-fd2a9d8a7a37_2856x272.png)**How does it feel to be 52 octillion times smaller than something?**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!g04N!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5db18906-d9c3-4951-8aa1-281a37b25710_2856x272.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!g04N!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5db18906-d9c3-4951-8aa1-281a37b25710_2856x272.png)You may be wondering, did I compare humans to gut bacteria?

Yes.

That is precisely what I've done.

We are in the universe's gut,

and we're part of a larger ecosystem.

An ecosystem which we know nothing about,

We observe our local environment,

and interact with each other,

maybe we leave Earth and interact with other planets,

maybe we voyage beyond the solar system,

to other galaxies, to other universes,

and we may interact with other planetary species.

But what happens when we make it out of the stomach?
