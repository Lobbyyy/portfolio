Comparison is the thief of joy. 

People say don’t compare yourself to others, but they don’t tell you how to do it. 

Introducing the **Flying Fish Framework.**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!P_sv!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3f240257-2bd5-4341-9d6a-fffb4d5d96a5_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!P_sv!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3f240257-2bd5-4341-9d6a-fffb4d5d96a5_1331x180.png)Have you ever seen a fish be sad because it can't fly?

or,

have you ever seen a bird be sad because it can't swim?

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!pYNR!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F46362bb7-006d-4dba-938e-2b4bbc69871a_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!pYNR!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F46362bb7-006d-4dba-938e-2b4bbc69871a_1331x180.png)You can compare a fish to a bird,

but what do you gain from that comparison?

They are different species;

they live in different environments

and have different adaptations to help them survive.

There is no use in comparing them.

It's futile because we judge them on their own merits and unique abilities.

You judge a fish by its ability to swim,

not its ability to fly.

You judge a bird by its ability to fly,

not its ability to swim.

A fish doesn't care about flying,

like a bird doesn't care about swimming.

Why should it?

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!bNgZ!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa2d26270-867c-42f4-8d27-7dfefbfdc068_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!bNgZ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa2d26270-867c-42f4-8d27-7dfefbfdc068_1331x180.png)A fish doesn’t have wings, or feathers or a beak,

and it doesn’t care about these shortcomings.

It doesn’t care that it can't fly.

It's a futile exercise for it to think about what it doesn't possess because it's not in that environment.

Having wings, feathers and a beak won't help it survive in the ocean.

Similarly,

a bird doesn't have gills, scales, or fins and doesn't care about these shortcomings either.

It doesn’t care that it can't swim.

It doesn't live in the water.

It spends its time in the sky and lives in the trees.

It's adapted to survive and thrive in its own environment.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!hAYJ!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6cc28778-5f60-41aa-9d3b-2cbfe7452e43_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!hAYJ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6cc28778-5f60-41aa-9d3b-2cbfe7452e43_1331x180.png)So, we judge a fish by its ability to swim,

and we judge a bird by its ability to fly.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mAsC!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdbcd36b3-fdcd-46d8-9504-87a9cc5ca210_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mAsC!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdbcd36b3-fdcd-46d8-9504-87a9cc5ca210_1331x180.png)We may even compare one type of bird to another kind of bird,

but is that any more useful?

There is some utility but only a little in the grand scheme of things.

Maybe you judge how high it can fly,

or how long it can fly,

or how big its wings are,

but even that is not useful if you're the bird.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!k4Ry!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F53a2dad0-107c-4426-8d41-39845c3861f4_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!k4Ry!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F53a2dad0-107c-4426-8d41-39845c3861f4_1331x180.png)**Subscribe for access to practical business frameworks and startup insights! 🚀**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5uA5!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8646f575-6251-45ff-a629-e57db87deb09_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5uA5!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8646f575-6251-45ff-a629-e57db87deb09_1331x180.png)Take two different types of birds - The Peregrine Falcon and the Great Horned Owl.

They are both birds;

both have excellent eyesight;

both have sharp beaks and talons;

and both are skilled hunters.

However, comparing them to each other has diminishing returns and, in the long run, has no utility.

Sure, you can draw up some comparisons:

The average wingspan of a falcon is 3.3-3.6 ft, while an owl's is 3.9-4.6 ft; 

falcons fly at 240 mph during hunting dives, while owls fly at 20-40 mph;

a falcon can fly at altitudes of 3,000 ft, while owls usually fly below 100 ft.

These may help you draw some insights into the characteristics of each bird.

But you don't care about the owl if you're the falcon.

And if you're the owl,

you don't care about the falcon.

Both are birds and may have similar characteristics, physical or otherwise. 

Still, from the perspective of the owl or the falcon,

you don't care what the other one does.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wzUM!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb36b11dd-884b-41c6-a4df-f0d20ebeabe9_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wzUM!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb36b11dd-884b-41c6-a4df-f0d20ebeabe9_1331x180.png)If you're the owl, you don't care about the falcon's wingspan,

or that it reaches altitudes of 3000 ft,

or that it flies 6-12 times faster.

The only thing you care about if you're the owl is the habitat you live in,

and the adaptations you possess to help you survive in that environment.

You care that you have exceptional night vision,

you care that your specialised feather edges give you the ability to fly silently,

you care that your asymmetrical ear placements help with precise sound location.

Similarly, if you're the falcon, you don't care about an owl's ability to see in the dark

or to fly silently,

or where your ears are placed.

You care about your abilities

and how they help you hunt and survive in your habitat.

You care that you have exceptional depth perception and can spot prey from a mile away;

you care that your body can handle the incredible forces of descent;

you care about your third eyelid, which protects your eyes during high-speed dives.

Both are birds and belong to the same species,

but are unique.

They are both unique in their own way.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!shrz!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F383f5d4c-941f-4ccb-8927-f6090d8c0f29_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!shrz!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F383f5d4c-941f-4ccb-8927-f6090d8c0f29_1331x180.png)Just like how comparing across classes - a fish to a bird, is a futile exercise,

and comparing different orders within the same class - like a falcon to an owl - brings no value,

comparing yourself to other humans is an equally pointless exercise.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Vhuz!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fde8ebbb1-8917-4f9d-be68-434727fc3958_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Vhuz!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fde8ebbb1-8917-4f9d-be68-434727fc3958_1331x180.png)[Share](https://deckandadream.substack.com/p/flying-fish-framework?utm_source=substack&utm_medium=email&utm_content=share&action=share)

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Sd6e!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9eda19d9-bb80-4aa2-8efb-60528768aece_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Sd6e!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9eda19d9-bb80-4aa2-8efb-60528768aece_1331x180.png)Humans are special; what makes us unique is our mammalian brain.

Our abilities to reason, communicate and create tools make us unique from other animals.

This is the first level of difference.

The same type of difference that makes a bird different from a fish,

or a kangaroo different from a penguin.

There is also a second level of difference within our own species.

The same type of difference that makes a falcon different from an owl or a tiger different from a lion.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Mq1e!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1de7af03-1845-4748-99d8-5ace66e6374d_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Mq1e!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1de7af03-1845-4748-99d8-5ace66e6374d_1331x180.png)Each human is unique.

There are 8 billion of us, and each one is different.

It may not feel like it given that there are so many of us,

but each one of us is special.

We may be from the same country,

speak the same language,

celebrate the same traditions,

walk the same streets,

breathe the same air,

share the same culture,

or even live under the same roof,

but every single one of us has a different genetic code, as distinct as our fingerprints.

We have unique upbringings,

unique experiences,

unique memories,

unique talents and abilities,

unique dreams and fears,

unique collection of friends and relationships,

unique ways of expressing joy and sadness,

and unique ways of thinking.

We are all unique and just like the owl or the falcon.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!8G3j!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9f6d32e8-9578-41e4-90d2-a1d5e3a12cd7_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!8G3j!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9f6d32e8-9578-41e4-90d2-a1d5e3a12cd7_1331x180.png)Applying the Flying Fish Framework is a good reminder of your uniqueness.

Sometimes, we're scared to be different and stand out from the crowd.

It feels safer to be a sheep and to go with the herd,

but you’re doing yourself a disservice by thinking that way.

It's okay to do what others are doing, especially if that is what you want to do,

but always know you are unique and don’t compare yourself to others because comparing yourself to others will bring you no joy.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!X8p3!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbebec414-0fe6-41cf-86c3-f10cf2ebe2d4_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!X8p3!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbebec414-0fe6-41cf-86c3-f10cf2ebe2d4_1331x180.png)It may bring you joy in the short run, but you'll be unhappy in the long run.

If you compare yourself to others, there are only two outcomes:

either you're doing better than someone else,

or you're doing worse than someone.

And you can apply this across any dimension of your choosing.

You may compare yourself to someone in one dimension and find out that you come out on top.

Great. Good for you!

This is equivalent to the Peregrine Falcon boasting of its ability to fly at high altitudes and belittling the owl.

But what happens if you compare yourself against another dimension?

How does the Peregrine Falcon fare when it's forced to hunt at night?

The owl will clearly outcompete the falcon in this dimension.

What does the owl or the falcon gain out of comparing itself?

Nothing.

The owl will win in some dimensions,

the falcon will win in others,

but neither is superior,

and both are special in their own way.

Both are unique and distinct, and each has autonomy over their lives.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Gzb7!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F83dac916-3d91-4490-b6c1-4790accce5ba_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Gzb7!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F83dac916-3d91-4490-b6c1-4790accce5ba_1331x180.png)We live in a big world,

and with the internet,

we're more connected than ever before.

We have a window into other people's lives, 

and comparing ourselves to someone else is easy.

But don't do it.

Use the Flying Fish Framework to remind yourself that you're a fish,

and they are a bird.

Don't diminish your uniqueness.

If you find someone similar to you, that you think is doing better than you,

use the Flying Fish Framework to remind yourself that you're a falcon,

and they are an owl.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!OTWI!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F185e0676-0dac-4d31-a50a-cdc0518930a1_1331x180.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!OTWI!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F185e0676-0dac-4d31-a50a-cdc0518930a1_1331x180.png)I love the Flying Fish Framework for a few reasons.

The first is that it's so practical and easy to apply

As soon as you start to compare yourself to someone,

be an animal - any animal you want, and make the other person a different animal.

The shift in perspective is instant.

You become the bird, and they become the fish.

You become a lion, and they become a tiger.

You become a zebra, and they become a giraffe.

You become a dragonfly, and they become a bee.

---

The second is that it keeps the human ego in check.

Superiority complexes shatter like porcelain on marble,

while inferiority complexes dissolve like shadows at noon.

With the shift in perspective, you quickly realise that you're not superior to others,

and others are not superior to you.

You are not better than them,

and they are not better than you.

A bird is not superior to a fish.

A fish is not superior to a bird.

A lion is not better than a tiger.

A tiger is not better than a lion.

---

The third benefit is how you treat yourself and others around you.

Once you appreciate your uniqueness, you appreciate other people's uniqueness.

It's a self-projection.

How you think about yourself is also how you think about others.

---

It has the added benefit of making you curious,

extremely curious.

Imagine a fish talking to a bird;

the fish would be amazed to learn how the bird flies.

Similarly, the bird would marvel at the fish's ability to swim.

If I was the fish, I'd want to learn how to fly

and if I was the bird, I'd want to learn how to swim.

In practical terms, this translates to more questions,

more dialogue,

more communication,

more respect,

more understanding,

and more empathy.

You end up caring more about people, and this energy is reciprocated.
