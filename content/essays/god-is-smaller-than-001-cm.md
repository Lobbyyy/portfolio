The notion was that humans aren't too different from the bacteria in our gut.

It got me thinking.

Thinking about God.

The consensus is that God is invisible.

We can't see God.

Yet,

we still believe in God.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Ng3o!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdbaa58d1-3fa8-4314-824c-ca7cc8d56f35_2858x420.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Ng3o!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdbaa58d1-3fa8-4314-824c-ca7cc8d56f35_2858x420.png)I'm not exploring the idea of whether we should believe in God or not.

I'm interested in something else:

**Understanding how big God is**.

If God exists in our universe

and we can't see God.

There are a few implications,

either

***Option A***: God is so tiny that our human eye can't see

or

***Option B***: God is so big that our human eye can't see

or

***Option C***: God exists on a different wavelength than we see.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ET-e!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8c3a3a1a-5b00-4874-999e-58eeed79bdcf_2858x420.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ET-e!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8c3a3a1a-5b00-4874-999e-58eeed79bdcf_2858x420.png)If God is so tiny that our human eye can't see,

then what can we use to see God?

A microscope?

No, that feels wrong.

God must be smaller,

way smaller.

Smaller than atoms?

Smaller than quarks?

hmmm

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!petX!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3a7c26cf-8365-440b-807f-c55eef8ade46_2858x420.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!petX!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3a7c26cf-8365-440b-807f-c55eef8ade46_2858x420.png)Subscribe if you think option A 🎉

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!POH2!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd3288079-14fd-4c89-882d-45aa184f82f4_2858x420.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!POH2!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd3288079-14fd-4c89-882d-45aa184f82f4_2858x420.png)If God is so big that our human eye can't see,

then whatever we see is just a part of God.

Our observable universe - all 8,800,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000 meters of it,

is just a fraction of God's true scale.

Like how you would only see part of an elephant if it was standing right next to you.

If I carry on the gut analogy, God could be so big that we're part of its body.

God is us.

Walking, talking,

and living life,

maybe God is studying for an exam,

or hosting a dinner party.

God could be doing anything.

Maybe it's all happening in heaven.

Who knows?

hmmm

somewhere on the electromagnetic spectrum.

Humans can only see wavelengths between 380-700 nanometers on this spectrum.

That's less than 0.0000001% of the total electromagnetic spectrum.

So, God could exist in the 99.99999% of the spectrum we don't see.

hmmm
