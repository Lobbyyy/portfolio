***Prelude***

If you haven't read Paul Graham's (PG) essay on [how to do Great Work](https://www.paulgraham.com/greatwork.html), you should.

It's a terrific essay, and this post has been inspired by it.

I've read it multiple times and keep returning to it.

PG is also why I started writing, so paying homage to his work feels right.

Thank you, PG!

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!P2lS!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4a2d44dd-4740-4e37-87e3-5f99c2b84ce0_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!P2lS!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4a2d44dd-4740-4e37-87e3-5f99c2b84ce0_1416x213.png)Over this summer, we were blessed with the sporting event of the year - the Olympics.

As a sports and athletics fan, I love watching people strive for greatness.

Each Olympian doesn't enter the competition to participate; they enter to win.

The most significant expression of this is the 100-meter sprint.

The winner gets crowned the fastest man or woman in the world.

The losers get nothing.

It's a game of margins.

It's a game of thrones.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!bgDl!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Faa29ae80-cda3-474c-8d4c-40fe6333ed27_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!bgDl!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Faa29ae80-cda3-474c-8d4c-40fe6333ed27_1416x213.png)In the finals, there are nine lanes.

One for each sprinter.

The sprinter has to stay in their designated lane. 

Otherwise, they get disqualified,

and because the margin for error is so tiny,

any deviation from the lane will lead to a significant loss in time and, likely, the race.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!O-MU!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbc1577af-c85c-4ccf-93f1-6322313dfad6_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!O-MU!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbc1577af-c85c-4ccf-93f1-6322313dfad6_1416x213.png)There is a clear incentive to 'stay in your lane'.

I view my career and professional life in the same way.

There is a lane that I've picked - entrepreneurship, and I'm staying within that lane.

I'm going as far and fast as I can down this lane because that's how I do great work.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sPIU!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb75a9493-cfff-435a-a9d1-d89f6d5f3f91_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sPIU!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb75a9493-cfff-435a-a9d1-d89f6d5f3f91_1416x213.png)**How do you find a lane to run in?**

By experimenting;

by trying out different lanes and seeing what running in that lane is like.

This is what I mean by 'first work wide'.

If you read [my story](https://deckandadream.substack.com/p/my-story) or [why startup founders should be more like thanos](https://deckandadream.substack.com/p/what-thanos-and-i-have-in-common), you'll observe that I have been constantly experimenting.

I've been constantly trying out different lanes to see if that is the lane I want to be on.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HPQc!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F61d9d1b1-cfd4-45ae-9325-b1de1e916791_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HPQc!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F61d9d1b1-cfd4-45ae-9325-b1de1e916791_1416x213.png)I tried the sales lane but could not see my future down this lane.

I tried the strategy consulting lane, and it was interesting. 

Still, it felt like a spectator rather than a track athlete.

I tried the VC lane, and I enjoyed it. 

But every time I glanced at the entrepreneurs in the lanes next to me, I felt a magnetic attraction.  

I wanted to be in that lane.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5V2m!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4aa84f87-e7af-48c7-a712-2d1dfc69ca10_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5V2m!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4aa84f87-e7af-48c7-a712-2d1dfc69ca10_1416x213.png)When you start your career,

you're starting your career for the first time.

In fact, can you believe that is your first time being human?

When you come out of the womb, 

or school,

or university,

there is a very low chance that you will pick the right lane for yourself. 

This is because of two reasons:

1) you are unaware of the lanes that exist, leading you to settle with your local maximum rather than your global maximum;

2) or you are unaware of yourself - your curiosity, passions and talents.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!PsZv!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3f98a2f6-db08-4590-a0b2-b2b61e5d03b9_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!PsZv!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3f98a2f6-db08-4590-a0b2-b2b61e5d03b9_1416x213.png)The remedy for 1) is research and networking.

The remedy for 2) is self-exploration. [Ikigai](https://open.substack.com/pub/deckandadream/p/do-you-want-a-long-and-happy-life?r=r7j0y&amp;utm_campaign=post&amp;utm_medium=web&amp;showWelcomeOnShare=true) is a great starting point for this.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!dwia!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7c3f854e-91dd-46cf-a442-d4c1de1834bc_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!dwia!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7c3f854e-91dd-46cf-a442-d4c1de1834bc_1416x213.png)Once you figure out the lane designed for you, it is time to 'work hard'.

Work hard and sprint down this lane;

go as far as you can go.

I say that working hard is the easy part because if you pick your lane correctly, 

you'll love what you do, and it doesn't feel like work when you love what you do.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!LMvk!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ffd3fe22a-1c74-44a8-b925-232877091860_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!LMvk!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ffd3fe22a-1c74-44a8-b925-232877091860_1416x213.png)So work wide, then work hard.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!JOxU!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F53c8b506-090c-41e0-ba43-b0f0b13884ce_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!JOxU!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F53c8b506-090c-41e0-ba43-b0f0b13884ce_1416x213.png)**Subscribe for access to practical business frameworks and startup insights! 🚀**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0WJJ!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F16982178-a895-4f63-9251-62e3daa7186e_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0WJJ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F16982178-a895-4f63-9251-62e3daa7186e_1416x213.png)If you do it the opposite way - working hard, then working wide, 

you may dedicate years of your life to work, only to discover that it amounts to nothing.

I'm not saying your hard work is for nothing,

but if your work is not meaningful to you, 

then it has no meaning.

You may get lucky and hit your global maximum on your first lane, but only some are this fortunate. 

**There is no point going a long way if it’s the wrong way.**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ii7d!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb0363b1f-dff8-4df2-91f7-511cf23a8d71_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ii7d!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb0363b1f-dff8-4df2-91f7-511cf23a8d71_1416x213.png)The goal is to find a lane that:

Speaks to your curiosity.

Speaks to your passion.

Speaks to your talents.

If these conditions are satisfied, you're more likely to go further in that lane,

and as a result,

you're more likely to do great work.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!efAc!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe63b5827-a07d-4284-a03b-a17c281fd0b5_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!efAc!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe63b5827-a07d-4284-a03b-a17c281fd0b5_1416x213.png)If you're early in your career, this is the first time you've run in any lane.

The only thing you can do is to pick a lane.

You may hate it, or you may get lucky.

The only way to figure it out is by trying.

If you don't like it, pick another lane and keep experimenting.

If you're later in your career, you've probably already run in a few lanes before,

or perhaps you've run in one lane for a long time.

Either way, you're more aware of the existing lanes and the lanes that could be right for you.

Get in those lanes and try them out.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HrSD!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F06a8d41c-a4fc-4da1-9377-b702cc56b2e7_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HrSD!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F06a8d41c-a4fc-4da1-9377-b702cc56b2e7_1416x213.png)**How many lanes are there?**

There are an infinite number of lanes you can run in.

Examples:

You can choose the marketing lane and be a marketing professional.

You can choose the finance lane and be a finance professional.

You can choose the medic lane and become a doctor.

Each lane can then be broken down into more lanes.

If you pick marketing, you could focus on content, paid, or influencer marketing.

If you pick finance, you can focus on being an actuary, working on the sales and trading floor, or in accounting.

If you pick the medic lane, you could be a cardiologist, dermatologist, or neurologist.

Each lane can be further subdivided into more and more narrow lanes.

As lanes become narrow, your work becomes more 'niche'.

Niche isn't a bad thing.

In fact, the more narrow the lane,

the fewer number of people likely to be in that lane,

and the more likely you are to be an expert in that domain.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!jNqI!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fafca13bc-6304-4df6-b984-5943ed0112cc_1416x213.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!jNqI!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fafca13bc-6304-4df6-b984-5943ed0112cc_1416x213.png)**When do you do great work?**

You'll also find others running beside you as you run along in your lane.

These will be colleagues, friends, and people in the same industry.

However, as you keep running, some people will start dropping off.

Maybe they reach the limit of their curiosity,

passions, and talents,

or perhaps they get tired,

or maybe they realise they aren't in the right lane.

Either way, their journey doesn't affect yours because you know your own lane.

You may reach the point when you look around and see no one around you for miles.

This is the first time anyone has gone this far in this lane.

I think of this point PG refers to as the 'frontier of knowledge'.

The point at which you become an expert in your field

and your work becomes record-breaking.

Your work becomes great.
