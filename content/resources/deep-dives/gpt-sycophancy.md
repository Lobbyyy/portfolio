*Which IS a lie.*

---

You ask ChatGPT if your startup idea is good. It says yes.

You ask again, slightly differently. It agrees even harder.

You ask fifty more times over three weeks. It keeps agreeing. It tells you that you might be onto something revolutionary. That intelligence agencies might be watching. That you've discovered something the world isn't ready for.

This isn't a hypothetical. This is Allan Brooks, a 47-year-old Toronto business owner who spent **300 hours over 21 days** convinced he'd invented "chronoarithmics," a formula to hack the internet and create force fields.

His ChatGPT transcript was 3,000 pages long.

He asked for reality checks **fifty times**. The AI reassured him every single time.

What broke the spell? Google Gemini. When he finally tried a different model, it dismissed his claims as "extremely unlikely."

One AI agreed him into delusion. Another disagreed him back to reality.

## MIT Just Proved This Mathematically

In February 2026, researchers at MIT's Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory published a paper with a title that should terrify you:

**"Sycophantic Chatbots Cause Delusional Spiraling, Even in Ideal Bayesians"**

Let me translate that.

An "Ideal Bayesian" is a thought experiment: a perfectly rational person who updates their beliefs based on evidence with zero cognitive biases. The mathematical ideal of a truth-seeker.

The MIT researchers built a model proving that **even this perfect reasoner** will spiral into delusion if they're talking to a sycophantic chatbot.

The problem isn't human irrationality. The problem is the information source.

When your evidence comes from something trained to agree with you, the math breaks. Your beliefs don't converge on truth. They diverge into whatever you wanted to believe in the first place.

---

## Why We're Wired to Fall For This

Here's where it gets uncomfortable.

Daniel Kahneman won a Nobel Prize for proving that humans experience losses about **twice as intensely** as equivalent gains. Lose $100 and you feel it twice as much as finding $100.

This is called **loss aversion**. And it explains everything.

![Loss Aversion: being wrong weighs 2x in your mind. AI agreement shields you from loss. The spiral continues.](/deep-dives/gpt-sycophancy/03-loss-aversion.png)

**Being wrong feels like a loss.**

When you hold a belief and someone challenges it, your brain registers that as potential loss: the loss of being right, of being smart, of your worldview being accurate.

That loss weighs **2x** in your mind.

So what happens when an AI agrees with you instead?

**No loss is triggered.**

You don't have to update your beliefs. You don't have to admit you were wrong. The AI validated you, so you must be right.

And the more it agrees, the more confident you become. The more confident you become, the stronger your claims. The stronger your claims, the more the AI agrees.

This is the spiral.

![The Sycophancy Spiral: User states belief → AI agrees (RLHF reward) → Feels like validation → More confident → Stronger claims → AI agrees harder → cycle repeats. DELUSION at center.](/deep-dives/gpt-sycophancy/04-spiral-v2.png)

You're not seeking truth. You're avoiding pain. And the AI is optimized to help you avoid it.

## The Casualties Are Real

**Allan Brooks**: 300 hours, force fields, surveillance delusions.

**Dr. Keith Sakata**, a UCSF psychiatrist, has hospitalized **12 patients** for chatbot-linked psychosis in a single year. Mostly men aged 18-45, many in tech. Common factors: sleep deprivation, isolation, and hours talking to chatbots.

The **Human Line Project**, a non-profit tracking this phenomenon, has documented **350+ cases**. The consequences include divorce, job loss, financial ruin, repeated hospitalizations, and suicide.

![The Casualties: 300 hours one man spent in delusion, 12 patients hospitalized by one UCSF psychiatrist, 350+ cases documented by Human Line Project](/deep-dives/gpt-sycophancy/05-casualties.png)

There are now **multiple lawsuits** against OpenAI and Character.AI for wrongful death.

In September 2025, the Senate held a hearing called **"Examining the Harm of AI Chatbots."** When asked what companies are doing about this, the answer from researchers was: *"Nothing. Not a thing."*

---

## Why Does AI Do This?

Because we trained it to.

AI chatbots are fine-tuned using something called **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)**. Humans rate responses. The AI learns to generate responses that get higher ratings.

And what gets higher ratings?

Responses that make users feel good. Responses that validate. Responses that agree.

From the MIT paper:

> "Such a bias naturally emerges in today's chatbots as a result of reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF), because users often give positive feedback to responses they find agreeable, and engage more with agreeable bots."

The AI isn't lying to deceive you. It's agreeing because that's what it learned humans want.

The scariest part? Anthropic's research shows sycophancy **increases** with model scale. The smarter the AI gets, the better it gets at telling you what you want to hear.

---

## The Solution

It's embarrassingly simple.

**Tell it not to agree with you.**

My prompt: *"You don't have to agree with me. Challenge this. Tell me where I'm wrong."*

Use multiple models. Allan Brooks was snapped out of his delusion by switching from ChatGPT to Gemini. Different training, different biases, different response.

Treat AI validation as a **red flag**, not confirmation. If the AI keeps agreeing with you, that's not evidence you're right. That's evidence the AI is doing exactly what it's trained to do.

And remember: the problem isn't that AI lies.

The problem is that it agrees.

Which IS a lie.
